# Slide 5 Mentor Guide — Impact and Benefits

## Role of this slide

Slide 5 must answer:

> **What changes for the sponsor and end users if OceanTwin exists?**

Impact is not the number of features. It is the difference the product makes to a real workflow.

Strong SIH decks connect technical capability to a stakeholder, a before/after process and a measurable or inspectable outcome.

---

## What strong SIH winner decks teach us

Weak impact slides use generic claims:

- saves time;
- improves efficiency;
- scalable;
- user-friendly;
- useful for society.

Strong impact slides say **who benefits, what action becomes easier, and what evidence the prototype already demonstrates**.

The safest pattern is:

**Stakeholder → current friction → OceanTwin action → practical benefit**

---

## Primary OceanTwin stakeholders

### INCOIS / ocean analysts

Need:
- inspect model fields through depth and time;
- compare with in-situ observations;
- retain scientific context and source traceability.

OceanTwin benefit:
- one browser workflow instead of separate visual / file-analysis steps.

### Researchers / data scientists

Need:
- interrogate full water-column variables;
- inspect profiles;
- identify residuals / statistical anomalies;
- access provenance and methods.

OceanTwin benefit:
- analysis and evidence context stay connected.

### Operational / policy users

Need:
- understand spatial ocean conditions without navigating raw scientific formats.

OceanTwin benefit:
- interpretable geographic and depth-aware views with source / QC context.

### Students / exhibitions / public science communication

Need:
- understand that the ocean is a 4D system, not a flat map.

OceanTwin benefit:
- geographic 3D + depth + genuine time + real instruments in a single visual narrative.

## Recommended impact layout

Use four stakeholder cards around one central statement.

Central statement:

> **From ocean data files to explainable, interactive ocean understanding.**

Each card should contain:

- stakeholder;
- current friction;
- OceanTwin action;
- benefit.

Keep each card to 2–3 short lines.

---

## Include measurable prototype evidence

Impact should not be completely hypothetical.

Use a compact proof panel:

### Verified scientific evidence

**31** model depth levels  
**26** Argo profiles ingested  
**99** matched temperature levels across eligible evidence  
**50** matched levels in default demonstration profile  
**0.2254 °C** MAE  
**0.3188 °C** RMSE  
**3.851 km** spatial separation shown explicitly

These are evidence of analytical functionality, not claims of model superiority.

Label:
**Verified diagnostic demonstration window**

## Show the “before → after” transformation

### Before

**Separate model files**  
**Separate sensor data**  
**2D / specialist workflows**  
**Manual context switching**

### With OceanTwin

**One normalized evidence layer**  
**3D + full depth + time**  
**Clickable observations**  
**Comparison + provenance**

This is a stronger impact story than “faster and smarter.”

---

## Strategic sponsor alignment

OceanTwin should visibly align with the type of platform the SIH26067 problem asks for:

- browser-native scientific visualization;
- integration of numerical model and observations;
- depth and time exploration;
- multiple sensor types;
- extensible ingestion;
- scientific controls;
- standards/interoperability pathways;
- public/scientific communication value.

This slide can show that the product's impact comes from **integrating the requirement families**, not from a single novelty feature.

---

## Scaling pathway

Avoid pretending the current bounded evidence window already equals an operational Indian Ocean service.

Use a three-step path:

### 1. Verified MVP

Bounded real-data windows, real observation evidence, working analysis workflow.

### 2. Operational breadth

Scheduled acquisition, persistent cache, broader time and regional windows, more source adapters.

### 3. Sponsor integration

INCOIS infrastructure alignment, monitoring, performance hardening, user feedback and deployment governance.

This is credible future impact.

---

## Optional “why it matters” statement

A concise closing line:

> **OceanTwin reduces the distance between a scientific dataset and an understandable, traceable decision-ready view.**

Avoid claiming it directly makes policy decisions.

---

## What not to do

Do not put:

- invented percentage time savings;
- invented user counts;
- fake cost savings;
- “millions of users” projections;
- unmeasured accuracy improvements;
- environmental benefits that the prototype does not calculate;
- emergency/forecast claims outside the PS and current implementation.

---

## Speaker intent

The speaker should connect one real workflow to one real proof.

Example:

> “For an ocean analyst, the value is not just seeing a colored map. They can move through depth, change genuine timestamps, open an actual sensor profile, compare it with the model and inspect exactly how that comparison was produced. For students or policymakers, the same scientific evidence can be presented in an understandable 3D form without losing provenance.”

---

## Acceptance criteria

Slide 5 passes only if:

- every benefit has a named stakeholder;
- at least one impact claim is tied to current prototype evidence;
- numerical evidence is correctly labeled as diagnostic;
- future scale is separated from current coverage;
- generic “saves time / cost” claims are either measured or removed;
- the slide makes the product's sponsor relevance obvious.

---

## Fail-safe rule

If an impact cannot be measured today, phrase it as a **workflow capability**, not a quantified benefit.

Example:

Bad:
> “Reduces analysis time by 70%.”

Safe:
> “Reduces context switching by keeping model, observation, depth/time controls and provenance in one workspace.”

---

## Final mentor test

For every card ask:

> “Who specifically benefits, and what can they do with OceanTwin that is harder in the fragmented workflow?”

If the answer is vague, rewrite it.
